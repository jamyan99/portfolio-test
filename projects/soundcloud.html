Overview

Spotify users who try SoundCloud get stuck on things they do without thinking: skipping a song, saving one, getting past an ad. Over 10 weeks, our team of three tested SoundCloud with 14 Spotify users, redesigned the moments that broke, and tested the redesign twice.

We worked as SoundCloud’s design team and finished with a recommendation to its product and engineering team: what to change first, and why.

My Roles

UX Research, Prototyping

My contribution

  • I designed and coded the interactive prototype we tested in both unmoderated rounds, then rebuilt it in the week between rounds based on what Round 1 showed.
  • I moderated 2 of our 5 sessions on the real SoundCloud app (P3 and P5).
  • The three of us planned the study, analyzed every session in a shared rainbow sheet, and wrote the recommendations together.

The Challenge

Spotify habits don’t work on SoundCloud

SoundCloud has music you can’t get on Spotify: new artists and songs that only live there. But a Spotify user opening it for the first time brings years of habits with them. Our brief was to find out how well SoundCloud serves these new users, and what it should change first.

Research Approach

Find the problems, then test the fixes

Each method answered a different question. The heuristic review told us where to look, the moderated sessions showed us why people got stuck, and two unmoderated rounds told us whether our fixes worked with no one there to help.

  1. Sep

    Heuristic evaluation

    Real app

    We reviewed the real app against usability heuristics: no skip buttons on the full player, two unskippable ads before the first song, artist links hidden behind “Show more.”

  2. Oct 9–17

    Moderated usability tests

    5 Spotify users · real app

    We watched people skip, save, find their saved music and get past an ad, and asked them why they did what they did.

  3. Nov 3

    Unmoderated test, Round 1

    5 users · our prototype

    Our first redesign, tested on UserTesting with no moderator, to see if the fixes worked without help.

  4. Nov 10–11

    Unmoderated test, Round 2

    4 users · revised prototype

    The same tasks and success criteria as Round 1, so we could tell whether our changes made things better.

Everyone we tested is a regular Spotify user who had used SoundCloud little or never. We recruited the moderated participants on YikYak and Instagram and screened the unmoderated ones on UserTesting. No task used the words skip, save, like or back, so we could see where people looked on their own.

Recommendations

What we asked SoundCloud to build

We ranked each fix by how often people hit the problem and how sure we were that the fix worked.

Now

Back, pause and next, plus one Save button

Both worked in both rounds, they’re cheap to build, and people use them on almost every song.

Next

Finish “Play after ad” and make the artist name tappable

Then test both again with new Spotify users, using the same tasks so the results compare.

Later

Retest everything with a bigger group

Our rounds were small, with 5, 5 and 4 people. The patterns repeated, but the numbers need a bigger sample.

The redesign we tested

Round 2 of the prototype I coded. It streams real songs through SoundCloud’s official embeddable player, so testers heard real music while they tried it.

Why it matters

For users

Skip and save happen on almost every song. When they work the way Spotify users expect, the first session stops feeling like a test.

For the product

A saved song is a reason to open the app again tomorrow, and an ad that doesn’t freeze the app makes the free tier feel less broken.

For SoundCloud vs. Spotify

Spotify users leave in their first few minutes when the basics break. These fixes keep them long enough to find the artists and songs they can’t get on Spotify.

What my part made possible

Because the prototype was code, I could rebuild it in the week between rounds: remove the heart, make songs tapped during an ad play next, and fix the bugs Round 1 found. That’s why Round 2 could test a real answer to Round 1 instead of the same design again.

Reflection

Easy wasn’t enough. By Round 2, all 4 testers called our redesign easy, but their interest in switching from Spotify averaged 5 on a scale of 1 to 11, mostly because of price and their music library. Fixing the habits keeps people in the app. It doesn’t win the switch on its own, and that’s worth knowing before anyone builds more.

What I’d do differently: run bigger rounds, test each round the same way, and pilot the prototype’s audio before testing. In Round 1, songs didn’t play for 3 of 5 testers because of an audio problem in the prototype, not the design. I fixed it before Round 2, but we lost some of what those sessions could have told us.

The biggest lesson for me: a fix isn’t done until people can use it without help. The Save button only worked once we took the heart away.
